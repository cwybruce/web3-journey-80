How Bitcoin transaction fees work

Bitcoin blocks have limited space (about 4M weight units), so miners pick the transactions that pay them the most per unit of space. Your fee is therefore not based on how much BTC you send — a $10 transfer and a $10 million transfer in the same simple transaction cost roughly the same fee. What matters is transaction size and how urgently you want it confirmed.

What is sat/vB?

Fees are priced in satoshis per virtual byte (sat/vB). One satoshi is 0.00000001 BTC. Your total fee is simple:

Total fee (sats) = transaction size (vB) × fee rate (sat/vB)

A typical single payment from a SegWit wallet is about 141 vB. At 10 sat/vB that costs 1,410 sats — a few cents when the network is quiet, more when it is busy.

Why do fees spike?

Fees spike when many people compete for the same block space — during big price moves, NFT-style inscription waves, or exchange withdrawal rushes. The mempool (the waiting room for unconfirmed transactions) fills up, and the minimum sat/vB needed to get into the next block climbs. Checking live rates before you send — like the estimator above — is the easiest way to avoid overpaying.

How to pay less

  • Use a SegWit address (starts with bc1q) — smaller transactions, lower fees.
  • Send when the network is quiet — weekends and off-peak UTC hours are often cheaper.
  • Batch your sends — one transaction with many outputs is cheaper than many transactions.
  • Consolidate UTXOs early — many small inputs make future transactions big and expensive.
  • Enable RBF (Replace-by-Fee) so you can bump the fee later instead of overpaying upfront.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a Bitcoin transaction fee right now?
It changes with network congestion. The estimator at the top of this page pulls current recommended fee rates (in sat/vB) from the Bitcoin mempool and converts them to sats, BTC and USD for your transaction size.
What is sat/vB?
Satoshis per virtual byte — the unit Bitcoin fees are priced in. Total fee = transaction size in virtual bytes × the fee rate you choose. Miners prioritize transactions offering a higher sat/vB rate.
How can I pay a lower Bitcoin fee?
Send when the mempool is quiet (often weekends), use a SegWit (bc1q...) address, consolidate UTXOs in advance, batch outputs, and choose the Economy tier if you are not in a hurry.
What happens if my Bitcoin fee is too low?
Your transaction sits in the mempool until a miner includes it — hours or even days during heavy congestion. If your wallet supports RBF (Replace-by-Fee), you can bump the fee afterwards.
Does the amount I send affect the fee?
No. Bitcoin fees depend on data size (number of inputs and outputs), not on the BTC value transferred. Sending $10 or $10 million in one simple transaction costs about the same.
